\section{Data Sources}
\label{sec:data-sources}
Technical recruitment often requires multiple data sources to be provided to evaluate candidates beyond traditional CVs, but are often rarely used. These sources have the potential to provide insights into the extensive professional history, technical competences, behavioural tendencies as well as real-world coding practices. Effective multi-sources screening will require a deeper understanding on the strengths and limitations of these data channels.

\subsection{Commonly Provided data sources}
The following data sources are commonly provided by candidates during technical recruitment processes:

\subsubsection*{Curriculum Vitae}
A CV remains the primary go-to document for recruitment pipelines, for technical roles it is especially important, allowing recruiters to quickly compare candidates without the need to commit to an extensive interview cycle for every candidate. 
A typical technical CV will include a candidate's name, education, employment history, projects and technical skills. 
Despite the amount of key data provided, research shows that CVs can contain unreliable self-representations due to intentional embellishment, omission, or fabrication \cite{henle2019_resume_fraud,dunning2004}. 
For this very reason, organisations increasingly supplement CVs with behavioural and performance-based evidence.

\subsubsection*{GitHub} 
For the vast majority of candidates applying for technical roles, GitHub is the most commonly provided data source 
besides the CV, only rivalled by LinkedIn. GitHub is a platform for hosting version-controlled repositories that
multiple developers can contribute to in parallel. For anyone in technical roles, github is, arguably the 
richest source of work-sample evidence. It can provide insights into the candidate's main tech stack (the languages
and frameworks they use on a day-to-day basis), their coding style, commit history and more. In HackerRank's 2018 
survey, around 80\% of small companies and approximately 66\% of large firms report placing a higher 
importance on portfolios \cite{hackerrank2018skills}. However, it is important to note that there are no
statistics on the depth of evaluation by recruiters. It is likely that review is inconsistent and may
be limited to the high-level elements such as READMEs, as opposed to the actual alignment of the candidate's 
profile to a role. Regardless of this, GitHub remains one of the most valuable sources of work-sample evidence.

\subsubsection*{LinkedIn} LinkedIn functions as a professional identity platform, it allows users to expand upon their projects, experiences and education, offering significantly more information than what may be provided on a CV. 
It also functions as a professional social media platform, where users can share their opinions on different topics. 
LinkedIn is widely used in recruiter workflows, in Jobvite's 2020 Recruiter Nation survey, 72\% of recruiters reported using or planning to use LinkedIn for recruitment \cite{jobvite2020}.

\subsection{Other data sources}
There are also other data sources that, while not often provided, can offer valuable insights into a candidate's suitability for a technical role. These include:

\subsubsection*{LeetCode} 
In preparation for Online Technical Assessments and Interviews, candidates will often practice short algorithmic problems
that replicate the problems they will encounter during their evaluation. One of the most widely used platforms for this is
Leetcode. In CoderPad's 2025 survey, we have that 49.8\% of employers use live coding interviews, many of which are either
from LeetCode, or in a similar style\cite{coderpad2025techhiring}. Candidate activity on these platforms demonstrate their
approach to solving problems as well as the topics of strength and preferred language. It may also expose areas where candidates
are less experienced, which could help recruiters assess adaptability to new or unfamiliar problems.

\subsubsection*{HackerRank} HackerRank is quite similar to LeetCode, in terms of it being a platform for coding challenges. 
It is also widely used as a platform to host online technical tests, setting it apart from its competitor. 
Many companies, especially those hiring at scale rely on HackerRank for standardised assessments, making the platform an interesting data source for understanding a candidate's tested technical ability.

\subsubsection*{Stack Overflow} Stack Overflow is a Q\&A platform for software engineering knowledge exchange. 
Contributions on Stack Overflow can provide insight into a candidate's communication style, community engagement, and areas of expertise. 
Prior research shows that contributions on such platforms can correlate with professional experience and also domain specialisation \cite{vasilescu2013}.

\subsubsection*{Social Media} During background checks, organisations may review social flags, though this is often to identify red flags rather than evaluate technical competence. 
In a U.S. CareerBuilder/Harris Poll survey, 70\% of employers reported using social media to research job candidates during hiring \cite{careerbuilder2017_social}. 
Due to the practice being controversial due to privacy, bias and fairness concerns, MERIT will not consider non-professional social media as a supplementary data source.
Note that professional social media such as LinkedIn is still considered.

\subsection*{Relevance to MERIT}
As shown above, there are a variety of external data sources that can be used to evaluate a candidate's suitability for a technical role
beyond the traditional Curriculum Vitae (CV). Being able to capture and integrate multiple data sources can help provide more factors
to differentiate candidates between, allowing for a more comprehensive view of a candidate's suitability for a role. MERIT aims to 
integrate GitHub as a supplementary data source due to it serving as a strong indicator of a candidate's technical ability. LinkedIn
will also be selected as a supplementary data source due to it serving as a strong indicator of a candidate's professional experience.
Other data sources such as LeetCode and HackerRank are likely to not be selected as supplementary data sources due to the time 
constraints of this project, however, they are still considered as potential future data sources for MERIT.
